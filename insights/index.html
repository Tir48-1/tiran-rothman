This section brings together Dr. Tiran Rothman’s core ideas on behavioral finance, startup valuation, investing and financial freedom. It is designed as a knowledge hub: concise enough to answer a specific question, but connected to deeper topic guides, research and published work.

Behavioral finance

Good investment decisions require more than financial literacy. They require a process for dealing with attention, uncertainty, social influence and the emotional asymmetry between gains and losses. The behavioral-finance guide develops these mechanisms and translates them into practical decision rules.

Read the behavioral finance guide →

Startup valuation

Valuation becomes most interesting when the future is difficult to forecast. Early-stage companies force founders and investors to confront the assumptions behind growth, margins, capital needs and financing terms. The startup-valuation guide presents a framework based on scenarios, DCF logic, market evidence and capital structure.

Read the startup valuation guide →

Financial freedom

Financial freedom can be framed as the ability to convert accumulated capital into time and optionality. That requires more than maximizing returns: consumption, lifestyle adaptation, risk tolerance and the design of repeatable financial rules all matter. These ideas are being developed further in the forthcoming book How Do You Buy Time?
